# Using your lead assistant

For the business owner and office staff. No technical knowledge assumed.

---

## What this does, in one paragraph

When someone fills in the form on your website, the assistant texts them within
seconds, asks what is wrong, offers appointment times, and books one. You do
nothing. It works at 2am, on Sundays, and while you are under a house. Your job
is to check the dashboard once or twice a day for the handful of leads it
cannot handle alone.

---

## Signing in

Open the dashboard address and enter the password. It lasts **12 hours**, so a
morning sign-in covers the day. Each device needs its own.

If it ever says *"Dashboard is not configured"*, nobody has set a password and
it is serving nothing to anyone — that is deliberate, and whoever set it up
needs to finish the job.

---

## The three screens

### Dashboard

Six numbers, and one list that needs you.

| Figure | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Leads received** | New enquiries in the last 30 days |
| **Median response time** | How long a typical customer waited for the first text. This should be seconds |
| **Appointments booked** | With how many came in outside business hours — the ones you would previously have missed |
| **Booking rate** | What share of leads turned into a visit |
| **Messages** | Sent / received |
| **Awaiting first contact** | **Customers who have not been texted.** If this is not zero, read on |

Below that, **"Needs attention"** lists anyone still waiting for a first
message, oldest first. An empty list is a good day.

### Leads

Every enquiry, newest first. The preview column shows **what the customer last
said** — not what the assistant replied — so you can scan for the ones that
sound urgent or unusual.

Filter chips at the top narrow by status.

### A single lead

Click any name for the whole conversation, their address and phone, what they
originally asked for, and any appointment.

---

## The labels, and what to do about each

| Label | Meaning | Your action |
|---|---|---|
| **New** | Just arrived | None. The assistant is on it |
| **Engaged** | Replying, being qualified | None |
| **Booked** | Appointment confirmed | Make sure someone turns up |
| **Needs a human** | An emergency, or the assistant stepped back | **Call them.** This is the one that matters |
| **Appointment** | Has a confirmed visit | None |
| **No SMS consent** | They did not agree to be texted | **Phone them instead.** Nothing may text them |
| **Awaiting first text** | The first message did not go out | See *When something looks wrong* |
| **Opted out** | They replied STOP | Nothing. Do not text them again — this is the law, not a preference |

## What it does without asking you

**Answers instantly.** Every lead gets a first text within seconds of the form
being submitted.

**Spots emergencies before anything else.** Gas smells, no heat in freezing
weather, carbon monoxide, flooding — these are matched by wording, sets the
lead to **Needs a human**, and **texts you directly** so you can call. This
happens even if the AI service is down, because it is not the AI making that
decision.

**Books appointments.** It offers your configured time slots, takes a numbered
reply, and confirms. Two people cannot take the same slot on the same day, even
if they reply at the same instant.

**Cancels and reschedules.** "Cancel my appointment" releases the slot; "can we
move it" releases it and offers new times.

**Honours STOP and HELP.** STOP stops everything to that number, permanently
and immediately. HELP gets a fixed reply naming your business and how to reach
a person.

---

## What it will never do

- **Text anyone who has not agreed to it.** No consent on record, no message.
- **Text anyone who replied STOP.**
- **Tell a customer they are booked when they are not.** A confirmation is only
  sent after the appointment exists.
- **Decide an emergency by itself.** That is fixed wording, checked in code.
- **Invent your prices, your service area, or your availability.** If it was
  not configured, the assistant does not claim it.

## Your daily two minutes

1. **"Awaiting first contact" — is it zero?** If not, those people are sitting
   there unanswered.
2. **Any "Needs a human"?** Call them. That label exists because something
   needed a person.
3. **Glance at the newest leads.** The preview shows the customer's own words,
   so anything odd stands out.

That is the whole routine. Everything else runs itself.

## When something looks wrong

**"Awaiting first text" on several leads.**
The texting service is not sending. Usually the gateway, not the assistant. The
leads are safe and stored — nothing is lost — and they can be texted once it is
fixed. Ring the ones that look urgent in the meantime.

**"No SMS consent" on every new lead.**
Your website form has stopped sending the consent tick. Whoever looks after the
site needs to fix it, and until then those customers must be phoned. Leads are
still captured.

**A customer says they never got a text.**
Open their conversation. If it shows as sent, it left the system and the
problem is between the carrier and their handset. If it does not, see the first
item.

**The dashboard will not load.**
It only reads data — nothing is lost and nothing is changed. Try again; if it
persists, whoever set it up needs the server log.

**Someone replies STOP by mistake.**
They must text **START** themselves. Nobody can re-subscribe them from this
side, and that is intentional.

---

## Questions worth asking whoever set this up

- What are our configured appointment slots, and how long is a visit?
- What counts as an emergency, and whose phone gets the alert?
- What exactly does the first text say?
- What happens outside business hours?

All of those are settings, not code. They can be changed without rebuilding
anything — so if the wording is not how you would say it, say so.
